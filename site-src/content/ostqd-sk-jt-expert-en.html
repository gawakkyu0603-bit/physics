Observer-separated open dynamics in dilaton gravity: technical overview

A guide to the manuscript's conditional hypotheses, analytic results, quantitative benchmarks, and scope.

TL;DR

The follow-up relaxes subsystem BPS immunity to thermal Layer 1 open systems. Two focused additions are the all-order frozen-coefficient Bernard–Doyon JT cumulants and fixed-drift three-tier equilibrium comparison, and the degree −2 smooth-band chiral defect with a necessary finite-order point-contact positivity floor. KMS covariance, two-time curvature, Ward identities, regression, bosonic modes, port locking, greybody scattering, and channel bottlenecks are conditional supporting results. No global continuum positive state, microscopic rate, selective Layer 2 instrument, or common gravitational endpoint is constructed.

Framework, hypothesis change, and attribution (§1)

This study follows finite-dimensional Observer-Separated Two-Time Quantum Dynamics (OSTQD). The previous strict self-perspective BPS condition [L_k,H]=0 is relaxed for Layer 1 thermal reduced dynamics, not preserved while deriving nonzero energy flux. The two-time parameters t and s designate reversible evolution and non-selective dissipative flow. Neither a microscopic s(t) nor a selective instrument family is derived. The paper pairs finite-dimensional models with a low-energy 2D JT+CFT saddle, a free chiral-boson diagnostic, static 4D greybody scattering, and separate finite channel benchmarks.

Directly inherited results include Schwinger–Keldysh/Feynman–Vernon normalization, Davies covariance and detailed balance, Evans–Frigerio Dirichlet kernels, Bernard–Doyon counting statistics and exchange symmetry, JT evaporation and Schwarzian thermodynamics, Sperling–Vogel phase-diffused squeezed states, and established greybody and non-isometric-code results. The two focused analytical additions are the frozen-coefficient cumulant/three-closure comparison and the smooth-band chiral distribution/point-contact classification. The remaining statements are adaptations, conditional applications and controls.

Preceding finite-dimensional formulation.

Influence action, positivity, and unraveling gauge (§2)

Write O_r=(O_++O_−)/2 and O_a=O_+−O_− for a stationary bath coupled through gO⊗B. With ρ_spec=−2 Im D_R, G^>−G^<=ρ_spec and N=(G^>+G^<)/2, the quadratic influence functional uses the following convention.

\[S_{\rm IF}^{(2)}=-g^2\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}O_a(-\omega)D_R(\omega)O_r(\omega)+\frac{ig^2}{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}O_a(-\omega)N(\omega)O_a(\omega)\]
\[D_K=2N=\coth(\beta_H\omega/2)\rho_{\rm spec},\qquad\gamma_\downarrow=g^2 G^>,\quad\gamma_\uparrow=g^2 G^<\]

The normalized, conjugation-symmetric, nonnegative-imaginary SK conditions are necessary for unitary dilation, not sufficient for CP. A Schur multiplier with diagonal 1 and off-diagonal 0.9,0.9,0.1 has minimum eigenvalue −0.0745913. The 80-level bath calculation checks the noise coefficient and nonzero retarded controls. The QND reduction to a purely dephasing generator presumes the appropriate Markov and zero-frequency limit; it is not a general deletion of the retarded kernel.

For a fixed uncorrelated bath (or specified CP assignment), trace-out is a linear non-selective CPTP channel. Orthogonal mixing of two Kraus operators preserves the channel yet changes conditional states and probabilities (F1: p₀=1 vs 0.36). This algebraic witness makes clear why Layer 2 outcome selection is not identified by Layer 1. No-signaling of the averaged marginal does not forbid conditional steering.

Theorem 1: thermal covariance, symmetric GNS and regression (§3)

For H time-independent and [H,L_k]=−ω_kL_k, covariance follows termwise because [H,L_k†L_k]=0. Energy-exchanging eigenoperators commute at the superoperator level with ad_H even though they violate subsystem BPS immunity.

\[[\mathcal L,\operatorname{ad}_H]=0,\quad \mathcal L^\dagger(H)=-\sum_k\omega_k L_k^\dagger L_k,\quad \Gamma_{\mathcal L}(H,H)=\sum_k\omega_k^2 L_k^\dagger L_k\]

For a faithful Gibbs state σ=S² and rates a²,b², aLS=bSL gives the symmetric intertwining L_pair(SXS)=S L_pair†(X)S for every X. The Hermitian relative density B=S⁻¹ρS⁻¹−I satisfies the dual equation. The χ² norm is Tr(σB²), not the squared GNS norm of a non-Hermitian left quotient. The stationarity Dirichlet identity gives

\[\partial_s\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma B_s^2)=-a^2\|[L,B_s]S\|_F^2-b^2\|[L^\dagger,B_s]S\|_F^2\le0\]

F2 checks noncommuting states, cooling purity 0.166667→0.504772, symmetric divergence 398.733→0.355539, and covariance residual about 1.77×10⁻¹⁵. The two-level non-secular Redfield Kossakowski determinant −(γ_down−γ_up)²/4 is negative for unequal positive rates. It is an obstruction for the specified unprojected generator, not all non-secular CP constructions.

For faithful σ and strictly positive rates, the initial real derivative discrepancy between C_QRT(τ) and the uncoupled C_free(τ) is the negative weighted Dirichlet form. Equality forces commutation with both jumps. This Evans–Frigerio criterion is deliberately not a statement comparing regression with the interacting system–bath unitary C_total; Markov dilations can realize agreement with C_total.

Proposition 1: two-time curvature and relaxation (§3)

For ∂_tρ=Kρ and ∂_sρ=L(t)ρ with fixed K, integrability is ∂_tL−∂_sK+[L,K]=0. Even when instantaneous covariance holds, a changing generator produces local curvature. The difference between the two endpoint paths around a rectangle admits an exact Duhamel bound because unitary conjugation is a trace-norm isometry and CPTP propagators are contractions.

\[\|(\Phi_{\rm top}-\Phi_{\rm bot})(\rho)\|_1\le\Delta s\|\mathcal L(t+\Delta t)-\mathcal L(t)\|_{1\to1}\le\Delta s\Delta t\sup_\tau\|\dot{\mathcal L}(\tau)\|_{1\to1}\]

Distinct endpoints with equal generators can have zero finite-rectangle discrepancy despite nonzero intermediate curvature. For a moving faithful instantaneous stationary state σ_t, the entropy derivative has a nonpositive dissipative contribution and the term −Tr(ρ_t∂_t log σ_t), with indefinite sign. Instantaneous stationarity is required for the first sign. A small bath-variation parameter is insufficient to imply Gibbs tracking without μ/λ_rel≪1.

Theorem 2: composite Ward identities and chiral JT closure (§4)

The composite mean Ward identity L_tot†(H_BH+H_rad)=0 entails flux balance, not vanishing carré du champ. Commuting total jumps are equivalent to first/second-moment immunity. The three-level jump example in the manuscript has H=diag(0,1,2), L_± transitions from the middle level to the outer levels, zero drift, Γ=2|1⟩⟨1| and variance 1−e^(−2s).

The outgoing canonical chiral current at positive ω has ρ_spec=ω/2 and rates g²ω(1+n)/2, g²ωn/2. Coupling equally to outgoing thermal and ingoing vacuum sectors yields ratio n/(2+n), not a single KMS law. Parameter-free semiclassical gravitational drift is distinct from matter rates requiring g and an actual bath/observer algebra.

For a JT saddle with C,c>0 and vacuum bath, E=2π²CT², S_BH=S₀+4π²CT, k=c/(48πC), γ_E=2k. Then T=T₀e^(−ku), E=E₀e^(−γ_Eu) and E'=−πcT²/12. The outgoing entropy rate is πcT/6. The two-channel equilibrium white-noise strength is πcT₀³/3, hence Var_eq=4π²CT₀³. The disk one-loop correction adds 3T₀²/2. The vacuum-bath Gaussian variance solves V'+2γ_E V=πcT³/6:

\[V(u)=8\pi^2CT(u)^3(1-e^{-ku}),\qquad \frac{\sqrt V}{E}=\sqrt{\frac{2(1-e^{-ku})}{\pi^2CT}}\]

The distinct control indicators CT≫1, k/T≪1 and √V/E≪1 separate large-C, adiabatic and linearized assumptions. With C=12, c=24, S₀=20, T₀=1, CT=1 occurs at u=187.3575 (√V/E=0.43099); the relative fluctuation, adiabatic and formal breakdown thresholds are 308.9793, 325.9302 and 412.2408. The Gaussian Monte Carlo uses the exact one-step variance, avoiding discretization bias; the recorded relative variance error near CT=1 is 0.3069%. Late times outside control remain formal extrapolations.

Proposition 2: cumulants, feedback and Hamilton–Jacobi factorization (§4)

The inherited Bernard–Doyon chiral CGF is α[(β−z)⁻¹−β⁻¹], α=πc/12. On the deterministic saddle, q_n=α n!T^(n+1), and the centered outgoing process contributes (−1)^n q_n to the cumulants of energy deviation. For the linearized additive closure δE' with drift −γ_E δE and all K_n(0)=0, integrate K'_n+nγ_EK_n=(−1)^nq_n to obtain

\[K_n(u)=(-1)^n\frac{n!}{n-1}4\pi^2C\,T_H(u)^{n+1}(1-e^{-(n-1)ku}),\quad n\ge2\]

The nonhomogeneous compound-Poisson representation has rate αT and exponential jump mean T. It is an exact representation of the frozen Markov CGF, not a microscopic independent-increment law for δE. At CT=1 the theoretical skewness is −0.1910 and the excess kurtosis 0.06026. Exact-time simulation with 10⁵ paths gives K₃ relative error 2.18%; at the formal late threshold, K₂,K₃,K₄ errors are 0.50%,0.72%,0.15%.

Restoring T(E)=√(E/A_C), A_C=2π²C, gives D_n(E)=(−1)^nα n!T(E)^(n+1). The term 3D₂'(Ē)K₂ changes the leading third cumulant at the same O(C) order over u=O(C). With r=e^(−ku), K₃^{state,lead}=6A_C T⁴(1−r)(5−7r); the sign changes for r<5/7. Thus the frozen result must not be treated as the universal leading evaporating skewness.

Theorem 3 and Proposition 3: chiral horizon distributions (§5)

The phase-diffused squeezed state is inherited from Sperling–Vogel. For arbitrary finite Schmidt dimension N, ρ_ij(s)=c_i c_j exp[−Γs(i−j)²/2] solves the number-dephasing ODE entrywise. For tridiagonal H_A the expectation changes by its diagonalized-minus-initial difference times 1−e^(−Γs/2). At fixed positive frequency the Fock remainder is O(Nq^N), q=e^(−βω); convergence is not uniform at ω=0.

\[\langle H_A\rangle=4\omega n_\sigma(1+\sigma n_\sigma)(1-e^{-\Gamma s/2}),\quad \sigma=\pm1\]

For the free outgoing c=1 current in Kruskal coordinates, W_U=−[4π(x−y−i0)²]⁻¹. On xy<0, dephasing modifies only cross-horizon correlations. Define f(ω)=ω(1−e^(−Γ(ω)s/2))/sinh(πω/κ) and K(z)=∫f(ω)cos(ωz)dω. The raw defect is

\[\Delta W(x,y)=\frac{K(\kappa^{-1}\log(|y/x|))}{4\pi\kappa^2|xy|},\quad xy<0\]

For symmetric splitting the defect is C_Γ/(4πκ²ε²)>0. Every extension agreeing with the off-horizon kernel is non-Hadamard by smooth-difference regularity; the coincidence-limit stress tensor is not assigned a finite value. Same-side correlators and the asymptotic outgoing flux remain unchanged. Uniform Γ gives a raw logarithmic cutoff coefficient (1−e^(−Γs/2))/(2π).

For smooth nonnegative nonzero Γ∈C_c^∞((0,∞)) and s>0, f vanishes near zero and K is Schwartz. Combine the two cross-horizon quadrants using radial r=|x|+|y| and log ratio z. The r⁰ test-function term cancels because ∫K(z/κ)dz=0; the r¹ term cancels by parity. The remaining radial integrand is O(r), defining the raw tempered distribution without counterterms. For Gaussian switching, subtract the zero angular integral to obtain the finite raw response. Twice integrating by parts gives

\[\Delta\mathcal F_{\rm raw}(\Omega;\sigma_\tau)=-\frac{A}{\Omega^2}+O(\Omega^{-4}),\quad A=\frac{1}{2\kappa^3\sigma_\tau^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{\omega^2(1-e^{-\Gamma s/2})}{\sinh^2(\pi\omega/\kappa)}\,d\omega>0\]

The vacuum Gaussian response decays faster, so the total raw response is strictly negative at large gap. This differs from the uniform-spectrum radial divergence and from other distributional detector prescriptions. For a finite-order contact perturbation Σ c_mn δ^(m)(x)δ^(n)(y), degree −2 homogeneity of the background makes the dilated Gaussian test at fixed a=Ωσ invariant. Taking σ→∞ removes all higher derivative contacts, requiring

\[c_{00}\ge -\inf_{a>0}\{\mathcal F_U(a)+\Delta\mathcal F_{\rm raw}(a)\}=c_{0,\min}>0\]

The manuscript then tests jets at small dilations: the highest active homogeneous contact order must be positive semidefinite, eliminating indefinite leading blocks; the resulting fixed-width high-gap response has a non-decaying lower floor (or higher-order growth). F4 finds a numerical witness −F₀(2.1501)≈0.00197949 and smooth-band point-split slope −2.0. The witness is model-specific; it is not an observed constant. Positivity sufficiency for arbitrary tests, axis-supported modifications away from the origin, and existence of a global continuum state remain open.

Proposition 4: state restriction, instrument scope and record design (§5)

With S_IF^A=0 and Unruh input by assumption, the separated fixed-s construction preserves the exterior state restriction because the right reduced density is unchanged. When s=s(t), the regression correlation for b_R acquires e^(−ΓΔs/2) relative to the free-subsystem correlation; this is not a general total-system no-go.

For number-projective outcome |n,n⟩ the inertial energy is ω[(2n+1)cosh(2r)−1]≥2ωn_BE>0, uniform over outcomes of that instrument only. Its probability average reproduces the full ensemble heating at s→∞. Uniform phase-unitary unraveling of the same complete dephasing yields branch energy 4ωn(1+n)(1−cosθ), with infimum zero. On the truncated Schmidt space noncommuting P_A,P_B rule out a common exact vacuum with sharp exterior projector eigenvalue and joint PVM; common (0,0) eigenstates can exist for N≥3. This is not the full Wigner's-friend or local-friendliness theorem.

Non-selective linear CPTP dynamics preserves the remote marginal; conditional remote steering is allowed. Forbidding conditional remote collapse and specifying cross-perspective record links are distinct additional design postulates. The two-mode inertial H_A is not a local stress-tensor signaling observable.

Theorem 4 and Proposition 5: condition-indexed restrictions (§6)

For any fixed coupled port with g≠0, T,ω>0, KMS relates ρ_spec=0 iff D_K=0 iff γ_down=0 iff γ_up=0. For g=0 one must formulate the relation using effective coupled kernels. Zero dissipation does not itself select the Boulware state; total emission requires summing all channels and the full Unruh state is not KMS. Assumed finite bipartite algebras commute with scalar intersection; obtaining a gravitational Cauchy-slice factorization and dressed algebras remains open.

The known static Schwarzschild low-frequency cross sections at fixed ω, A→0 for h=0,1/2,1,2 are A, A/8, ω²A²/(12π), ω⁴A³/(720π²), respectively for lowest allowed partial waves. F5 solves Regge–Wheeler and Dirac equations with stabilized Wright omega inversion f=δ/(1+δ), tracks Wronskian and matching controls and recovers these scalings. The fixed ωr_s=0.4 control gives scalar transmission 0.90893775; following Hawking-scale frequencies is not the fixed-frequency limit.

For a CPTP factorization through M₁, the encoder is trace and decoder is multiplication by a fixed density τ, giving exact F_e=1/d_C². This is the d_f=1 corollary of the earlier bound. Rank-nullity gives a nontrivial kernel for V from dimension 64 to 32; V alone is not trace preserving (null-state output trace ≈2.57×10⁻³¹). F6 keeps three benchmarks separate: a simplified JT entropy crossing (not QES extremization) with residual e^20≈4.85×10⁸, a static Haar post-selection map, and an isometric eight-qubit diary model with Petz recovery. For d_C=4,d_f=2 at k=7, F_rem=0.0660≤1/4; at k=8 F_rem=1/16. No common gravitational map V_u is proved.

Proposition 5 distinguishes causal access (future access specifically for points in J⁻(I)), per-port thermal locking, free-field cross-horizon regularity, and free-subsystem Markov regression. Each has different hypotheses; the synthesis motivates an open dissipative exterior boundary, not a universal closed-infall prohibition or unique endpoint.
