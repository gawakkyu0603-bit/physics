From quantum states to allowed routes

TL;DR — The main points

Proposal: Add irreversible gravitational selection of charge records to ordinary quantum correlations, and let those records change subsequent routes.

Result: Under a specified spherical constraint, the number of inward modes can be exactly zero. A finite example combines different receipts and quantum recovery within one preparation.

Tests: Compare reversal, interference, polarization, astronomical responses, distances, and expansion under stated assumptions for each branch.

Central unfinished task: Obtain the required conditions together in natural collapse, and connect different matrix sizes and clocks into one quantum theory. This is not a claim to have completed a theory of everything.

Correlation Actualization Theory (CAT) proposes adding an irreversible gravitational selection of records to ordinary quantum evolution. The selected record can then change the available routes. Using basic linear algebra and quantum mechanics, this guide separates the physical assumptions, their conditional consequences, and possible tests.

An infalling observer and an observer outside

A falls toward a black hole while B remains far away. They cannot receive all the same signals. In classical general relativity, A can cross the horizon in finite proper time. The delay and redshift of signals reaching B do not, by themselves, establish that A was destroyed at the horizon.

CAT adds the hypothesis that A can have an inward continuation while a surface ends that continuation in B’s physical description. It seeks to express this difference through received physical records and the output spaces those records allow, rather than through consciousness or the amount an observer knows.

How to choose an interaction

A gravitational tide couples to the quadrupole of a body’s mass distribution. A field that stretches along one direction and compresses along another can drive material vibrations or internal transitions. That exchange belongs to ordinary reversible physics if the source, field, and recoil are all included in the inverse.

Candidate mechanisms for transmitting a difference between layers include an independent mediator and a direct constraint at a received junction. Equal static forces need not imply the same arrival time, independent oscillations, self-response, or energy budget. The paper uses these differences as conditions on its choice of parent theory.

In particular, the demand that B’s inward route be exactly absent guides the action’s selection. Finite damping or a high barrier can make passage practically unlikely. If inward states remain allowed, however, the requested physical domain has not been obtained. The state-space argument below makes this distinction precise.

Distinguish a state from a quantum channel

A density operator ρ represents probabilities and the phase information responsible for interference. A quantum channel is a rule taking an input state to an output state. Let Kz describe the operation associated with outcome z. When outcomes are not selected, the evolution has the form

\[\mathcal E(\rho)=\sum_z K_z\rho K_z^\dagger,\qquad \sum_zK_z^\dagger K_z=I.\]

The condition on the right preserves total probability. The trace of a branch gives its probability; dividing that branch by its probability gives the conditional state. The dagger denotes the conjugate transpose. This mathematical form alone does not establish fundamental irreversibility: an ordinary environment whose correlations are ignored can also produce such a description.

CAT postulates that an occupied gravitational contact selects a charge record and irreversibly removes coherence between different charges. On the same fixed network, A and B may classify records at different levels of detail, but summing all outcomes gives the same total channel. Different physical routes therefore require an additional specification of the output spaces.

An unlikely route and an absent route

At a common input port, suppose B’s mixed state contains A’s state with positive weight. Mixing alone cannot make a route strictly impossible for B if it is possible for A. Even ten independent, balanced binary comparisons leave a passage probability of 1/1024 when passage requires agreement in every comparison.

CAT therefore imposes a physical constraint at a spherical junction. Let n and m be the integers associated with its two received ends. Counting sections over the sphere gives the number of inward spatial modes in the specified polarized sector:

\[N_{\mathrm{in}}=\max(1+m-n,0).\]

For n equal to 2, the choices 1, 2, and 3 for m give zero, one, and two modes. One spatial mode can carry an entire unknown qubit. “One mode” does not mean “one known quantum state.”

The count is a mathematical consequence once the constraint is imposed. Requiring matter to obey that constraint is a physical postulate. Closing one port also does not establish the absence of B’s entire interior: that interpretation requires coverage of every inward route for the relevant species and energy band.

An outgoing recovery channel has a separate causal requirement. Information first received locally on a classical null event horizon cannot then be sent to infinity along an ordinary outgoing causal path. An emitting timelike boundary outside the trapped region, a receding boundary, or encoding into exterior modes before the final contact are possible implementations that must be specified separately.

A record needs a physical current

A contact is more than an abstract comparison. An excitation must arrive from a source, charge must be transferred, and the energy, momentum, and recoil must balance. Here a current means transport of physical quantities, not necessarily electric current.

In the example, a one-unit increase in the receiving end’s charge is accompanied by a one-unit decrease at a compensating end. Exciting the absorber also requires an energy source. CAT adds irreversible selection to this ordinary absorption and exchange process. A reversible charge transfer alone does not establish the proposed new selection law.

In the matrix receiver, a probe crosses an oriented three-dimensional part of the material while an eigenvalue passes through zero. An index counting spectral signs determines the signed charge transfer. This requires a material orientation, a spectral gap, and a transition that actually occurs; simply assigning an integer label is insufficient.

The signal must also arrive before the contact’s routing deadline. The proposal does not permit freely controlling a local device to change remote statistics. Causal consistency is checked in the explicit network of separated local operations; extending that result to general gravitational contacts remains unfinished.

The parent matrix theory

A parent theory is a more basic candidate description from which the low-energy junctions and scattering processes should arise. CAT adopts an IKKT-type ten-matrix action with fermions for ordinary reversible evolution. Its internal contraction has one time direction and nine spatial directions. The receipt rule itself does not derive this signature or the number of matrices.

In an appropriate clock sector, represent the time-direction matrix by a differential operator. The remaining nine matrices then evolve with the clock, giving BFSS-type matrix quantum mechanics. Its bosonic action is

\[S_{\mathrm{clock}}=\frac{1}{g_1^2}\int d\tau\,\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\frac12(D_\tau X^i)^2+\frac14[X^i,X^j]^2\right].\]

Dτ is the time derivative including the gauge connection, brackets denote a matrix commutator, and g1 is the coupling. A commutator of Hermitian matrices is anti-Hermitian, so the trace of the second term is nonpositive. Its displayed plus sign in the action should not be mistaken for negative physical energy: the corresponding bosonic Hamiltonian is nonnegative.

To evolve a density operator, the full prescription uses two branches for an amplitude and its complex conjugate, inserting selection and routing at contacts. Empty intervals retain ordinary unitary propagation. Preserving total probability and conserving total four-momentum are separate conditions. The flat fixture satisfies the latter by including its source and support.

Histories and feedback from records

A history lists the receipts at successive contacts. A clock orders them, and only records already received may control the next operation. Summing histories that deliver the same record to an observer gives that observer’s probabilities and conditional states.

For routing, let a linear map Te describe the difference compared at contact e, and let ne count its physical occupation. The inward part is the kernel of the sum of squared differences:

\[C_{\mathbf n}=\sum_e n_eT_e^\dagger T_e,\qquad \ker C_{\mathbf n}=\bigcap_{e:n_e>0}\ker T_e.\]

Every term is nonnegative, so a mismatch in one comparison cannot cancel a mismatch in another. Components with zero difference in every occupied comparison continue inward; the remainder go to the surface route. Both routes can carry the same internal quantum state. Mutually exclusive configurations occupy separate blocks: a tiny probability of another configuration does not impose its constraint on all configurations.

Descriptions that exchange the clock order of independent contacts must preserve more than the channel after all outcomes are summed. Each retained outcome pair and the later control must agree. The supplement gives a single-qubit example whose inclusive operations coincide although the probability of two positive records is one half in one order and one quarter in the other. It distinguishes separated implementations from sequential measurements of the same qubit.

Infall sets a deadline for reception

Before the junction mathematics can operate, an actual collapse must prepare the necessary signals and charges. In the paper’s ten-solar-mass example, falling from 19.2 to 1.2 Schwarzschild radii takes 5.44 milliseconds on the infaller’s clock. Another 20.66 microseconds remain before the horizon.

In a separate calculation, an ingoing light signal travels from the reference crossing to a stationary receiver at 1.01 Schwarzschild radii. Only 1.003 microseconds elapse on that receiver’s proper clock between signal arrival and the infaller reaching it. The two margins use different clocks and endpoints; subtracting them does not give an available processing time.

Timely arrival and sufficient absorption are also different requirements. A specified cold, independent-molecule preparation gives only about 2.30×10−36 expected excited molecules for a 70 kg body. In homogeneous dust, the electric Weyl tide vanishes inside, leaving a quadrupole sensitive to that tide unexcited. Large mass alone therefore does not ensure a receipt.

A prepared matrix example with collective motion can change the index before the deadline, but its preparation supplies energy. The needed calculation must connect a source, arrival, absorption, compensating charge, and later contact from one natural initial state.

The stronger requirement of quantum recovery

Entangle the incoming qubit with an untouched reference. Recovering that entanglement from the output tests whether the unknown state’s phases survive. Identifying a zero or a one, or obtaining the correct total radiated energy, is not enough.

The paper applies quantum error correction to errors labelled by an accessible classical flag z and an inaccessible environmental alternative α. For a projector P onto the code space, the condition is

\[P K_{z\alpha}^\dagger K_{z\beta}P=c_{\alpha\beta}^{(z)}P.\]

The product on the left must act as the same scalar across the code space: information lost to the environment must not distinguish the unknown logical state. A flag may identify which type of error occurred. If the flag identifies the logical zero or one itself, recoverability changes.

A specified spin-one receiver absorbs two components with different probabilities, limiting the optimal entanglement fidelity of a qubit encoded in them to 0.930715. A fidelity of one means exact recovery. In another radiation example, changing relative phases while keeping intensities fixed changes emission from an adjacent-level difference from about 7.47×10−8 times the mean to about the mean. An intensity spectrum alone therefore does not determine a decoder.

The constructed exterior history transfers the code into photon-pair, graviton-pair, and residual-core carriers with weights one half, one third, and one sixth. Unavailable carriers must be treated as environment when checking recovery. The current semiclassical matching does not provide a complete solution through the last quantum of evaporation.

What does the probability 0.5376 describe?

The concrete example uses two receiving stations in flat spacetime and two successive pairs of packets from one source. Each station has a reference end and a comparison end. The first pair delivers absorbable polarization to both reference ends. The second delivers absorbable polarization to only one station’s comparison end.

Choose a source weight r of 0.75 and an absorption probability p of 0.64 for an absorbable packet. B closes when its reference end absorbs but its comparison end does not. Separate the 75% branch where the second packet is dark at B from the 25% branch where it is bright:

\[b=rp+(1-r)p(1-p)=0.64[0.75+0.25(0.36)]=0.5376.\]

The closure fraction for repeated preparations of this fixture is therefore 53.76%. This is a model value for a specified preparation, not a universal gravitational absorption rate or an observed measurement. Positive confirmation that both A ends absorbed coincides with B’s closure in 19.6608% of trials.

Next, inject one unknown qubit into the port appropriate to the observer being tested. A’s and B’s maps are alternative continuations of that input, not an operation producing two simultaneous qubit copies. On B’s closed histories, recovery is possible when the specified photon, graviton, and residual-core carriers are available. Recovery must preserve phases and entanglement with a reference, not merely identify a classical value.

Perfect recovery conditioned on closure differs from performance across every trial. In a separate benchmark with unit absorption but source weight three quarters, the whole-history entanglement fidelity is 13/16; losing access to the core lowers it to 23/32. On inward histories, B’s decoder receives an erasure flag instead of the quantum state.

What a finite calculation establishes

The reversible part of CAT uses ten Hermitian matrices. Ten counts the matrix components; it is separate from the size N of each matrix. Version 4 sets no universal maximum N. An explicit calculation chooses a matrix size, a physical clock, and a network of contacts.

Within that setting, it uses the specified scattering blocks with their full coupling dependence rather than retaining only low-order terms. This is the paper’s meaning of nonperturbative. It also constructs a finite sequence whose clock readings are material oscillator occupations, conditioning the state at each reading. This finite clock sequence is a different issue from taking the matrix size to infinity.

For the material clock, transferring one unit from one oscillator to the other advances the reading by one step. Write Φ for the operation taking the system forward one step and ρsource for the source. The operator Ω collecting the visited readings obeys

\[\Omega=\rho_{\mathrm{source}}+\Phi(\Omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Phi^j(\rho_{\mathrm{source}}).\]

Here N is the occupation defining the finite clock chain. Transfer vanishes past its endpoint, so the sum terminates. The trace of the full Ω counts visited slices; a state is normalized at a specified reading. No coordinate time is supplied at the outset, but the algebra, internal signature, prepared monotone clock sector, and source remain inputs.

It remains open how different sizes and clocks represent the same physical preparation and yield consistent probabilities. The finite examples and conditional Lean checks do not complete a theory of generic collapse, a continuum limit, and realistic matter and gravity.

From matrices to the geometry matter measures

Choosing matrices does not automatically establish a physical spacetime. The paper adds a small matter-wave probe and studies its propagation. The coefficients of second derivatives in the long-wavelength equation determine its light cone. In four dimensions an effective metric can be extracted from those coefficients.

The propagation cone and energy sign require separate checks. Version 4 aligns the real-time action conventions and verifies positive matter kinetic energy after clock reduction. This avoids accepting an unstable negative-energy wave merely because its equation suggests a useful geometry.

In an explicit wave-like matrix family, a condition setting the trace of the tidal matrix to zero selects a vacuum Einstein wave. The construction represents both polarizations and curved local geometry. However, not every solution of the matrix equations satisfies the Einstein equations. General matter backgrounds, a common light cone for all species, and stability must still be obtained together.

Beyond a singularity, and sharing a spacetime

Different continuations can agree on limited incoming data near the regime called a classical singularity. A rotating six-matrix example has an angular-momentum-dependent lower bound on volume and a smooth defocusing motion. Material oscillators nevertheless retain excitations, and angular modes have stability conditions. A smooth metric does not make the outgoing matter states identical.

Interpreting this example as generic singularity resolution or a vacuum transition would require a preparation from natural collapse and amplitudes between inequivalent vacua. The paper has not derived those amplitudes. B’s closed sector instead asks a different question: no inward state space exists beyond that contact.

A shared spacetime is tested by transporting and comparing clock ratios, light-travel times, polarization, and orientation. In two curved examples, orthogonal arms give the same round-trip times, but a 45-degree arm gives 0.33301 and 0.30801 in common normalized units. A single fitted value needs tolerance of at least 0.01250. Retaining orientation as part of the comparison matters.

Comparison maps must also agree on triple overlaps. The paper discusses possible inequivalent smooth structures in four dimensions, but proposes no rule assigning one exotic smoothing to each observer. Two observers within one solution and physically inequivalent vacua are different objects to count.

Counting vacua and stabilizing them

Matrix configurations, classical solutions, quantized fluxes, and stable quantum vacua are different sets. In the paper’s restricted torus-derived example, 48,055,489 flux configurations become 20,597 after an additional condition and 115 orbits under the stated equivalence relation. Of those, 49 fix the specified shape and coupling variables at the linearized level. These are not counts of all CAT vacua.

Matching differences between layer sizes can leave a common rescaling undetermined. If atomic masses depend on that common radius, it affects physical forces and clocks. The paper therefore compares the same background in ordinary CAT, IKKT, and static BFSS.

In the periodic-spinor sector examined, one-loop contributions cancel. All positive-energy free particles at external rest push the radius in the same direction. Mixing them with positive probabilities cannot create the missing oppositely directed restoring force. Bound states or additional receipt couplings require separate calculations.

A light, unscreened scalar coupled only through electromagnetism also predicts too large a difference between titanium and platinum falling accelerations to satisfy the MICROSCOPE constraint quoted in the paper. A viable vacuum must combine a stable radius, realistic matter, long-range gravity, and irreversible receipts. Measured constants have not been derived without free parameters.

What a reversal experiment would test

Suppose a pure initial state separates into orthogonal charge groups. If the ordinary environment and recoil can also be inverted, and the reversible operations within each group undone, CAT gives the following probability of returning to the initial state, in terms of the group weights pz:

\[F_{\mathrm{return}}=\sum_z p_z^2.\]

Two equally populated groups give a return probability of one half. A complete inverse of the corresponding closed unitary evolution gives one. To attribute a measured difference to the proposed law, ordinary dissipation, unrecovered environments, and apparatus errors must be independently bounded.

A separately designed logical-qubit readout calibrates absorption and both positive and negative reference inputs. Conditional on an error envelope covering every admitted input and the other stated controls, the return probability is at most 0.6596 for CAT and at least 0.9702 for ordinary inversion. This is a conditional experimental design. No gravitational apparatus has yet made a measurement satisfying those conditions.

Positive references alone cannot identify a detector that reports “return” for every input. The design uses 200 absorption trials, 300 positive references, 300 negative references, and 100 signals. Its absorption target is 8/9, separate from the earlier 0.64 preparation. Failed calibration and 86 or 87 signal returns are left undecided. Independent upper bounds of 0.01 are assumed for both the full circuit’s deviation from the calibrated ideal-output model and the dark-click probability.

The same distinction clarifies the time-reversal thought experiment. A traveller retaining a memory can identify a later visit even if a clock repeats its reading. Inverting ordinary operations, including memories, still need not restore a state after an irreversible gravitational record. This is an argument about comparable records, not a general classification of closed timelike curves.

Expansion and possible dark-sector applications

Specific matrix solutions must also be compared with the observed universe. One homogeneous spherical solution has principal expansion rates of opposite signs. The paper’s local Pantheon+ analysis uses 348 distance rows for 297 objects, giving normalized principal rates near 0.9792, 1.0003, and 1.0205. Under the homogeneity and low-redshift assumptions, this conflicts with extending that particular solution over the sample.

A different branch assumes a fixed acoustic ruler and exact coasting, with the cosmic scale factor proportional to time. Divide the speed of light by the Hubble rate, divide that distance by the acoustic ruler, and multiply by one plus redshift: the result must be constant. The paper compares DESI DR2 values 34.118±0.373 and 28.745±0.336 at redshifts 0.934 and 2.330, but treats them as a diagnostic selected after inspecting the data. It is not a full-likelihood comparison or an exclusion of late asymptotic coasting.

If some photons or gravitational waves follow a different route, inferred standard-candle or standard-siren distances can also change. The supplement’s example losing 10% of photon intensity gives a factor of 1.0541 relative to the ordinary distance relation. Losing entire events through selection requires a different statistical model.

Dark matter and dark energy are possible interpretations of particular stress components. Positive energy alone does not provide the inward force of a galactic halo or the required pressure. A model multiplying baryonic acceleration by a constant within each galaxy also encounters increasing outer residual fractions in 56 of 68 galaxies in the included two-annulus comparison. Cosmological applications require suitable matter distributions and dynamics in addition to a receipt law.

What is recalculated, and what is proved?

The release contains inputs, programs, reference outputs, formal proofs, and records of failed preparations. According to the Version 4 README, the standard numerical check compares nine programs, 59 data groups, and 1,195 floating-point values. The ten selected Lean modules contain 365 theorem or lemma declarations. These counts are not numbers of established claims about nature.

Lean’s kernel checks that algebraic or finite-sum conclusions follow from specified assumptions: examples include finite spherical-link columns, kernels of positive comparisons, finite-clock histories, and compositions of decoding maps. Physical polarization constraints, absorption implementations, telescope data, and the realization of the assumptions in generic collapse remain separate inputs.

Version 4 is an editorial revision. Its response describes the scope of hash comparisons against the previous version for computational inputs, reference results, programs, and Lean sources. Reproduction starts with the README’s Windows or Linux instructions. The numerical policy also distinguishes tiny nonzero values from numerical zeros.

The contribution statement identifies Yohtaro Soga’s concepts and research direction, Codex assistance with mathematical construction, literature, implementation, checking, and drafting, and separate AI-generated technical reviews. Readers can assess those roles alongside the calculations, proofs, and physical assumptions they can inspect directly.
