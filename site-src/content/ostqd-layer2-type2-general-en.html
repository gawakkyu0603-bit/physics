Quantum outcomes, observer records, and gravity

The third OSTQD study extends ensemble evolution to selective records.

TL;DR

An averaged channel does not fix individual outcomes. The paper establishes conditional steering obstructions, contextual Born records, a semifinite entropy split, sharp non-isometric bounds, and distinctions between causal dynamics and postselection.

These mathematical results do not derive a full gravitational instrument or a microscopic transition law.

From averaged dynamics to individual records

The first OSTQD study concerns finite-dimensional observer-indexed dynamics and information recovery. The second considers thermal open systems and free-field horizon correlations. Neither a density-matrix channel nor a Schwinger–Keldysh reduction specifies individual outcomes. A quantum instrument specifies outcome probabilities and conditional states, with the non-selective channel obtained by summing over outcomes. Many instruments can have the same sum.

The third study arranges observer instruments over a finite causal order and measurement contexts. Records that can later be compared have to agree under specified operations; incompatible measurement contexts need not admit one simultaneous assignment of facts.

1. A conditional state change is not a faster-than-light message

A local measurement of correlated quantum systems can change the conditional description of a remote system when the outcome is known. Ignoring the outcome leaves the remote density matrix unchanged. The paper proves that under an injectivity condition on the map from measurement effects to conditional remote states, forbidding every conditional change forces effects proportional to the identity: they reveal no input-dependent information.

The condition holds for full-Schmidt-rank pure states and some separable mixed states. Conditional state change therefore does not by itself demonstrate EPR steering in the multi-setting sense, entanglement, or Bell nonlocality. The rational CHSH witness S=14/5=2.8 rules out the stated positive four-outcome joint assignment under its assumptions. The specified Local Friendliness and global-section obstructions do not rule out all possible observer models.

2. Global assignments versus contextual Born weights

Model I insists on a joint assignment to all four possible binary outcomes. For S=2.8 a positive distribution is impossible. Allowing signed weights gives minimum negative mass 0.2. This is the inherited CHSH negativity result, not a newly discovered law.

Model II uses ordinary nonnegative Born probabilities within each measurement context. It requires separate causal agreement of records. In the premeasurement circuit a stable classical record can be copied with zero mismatch, while coherent interference distinguishes a superposition from its dephased version (probability 1 versus 1/2). These are different operations. A two-path example preserves the same record subalgebra without identifying every incompatible observable.

A specified normal affine assignment of weights is represented by a unique POVM, but this does not determine the weights from an ensemble channel. A relativistic system of record-transfer maps remains open.

3. The entropy of the mixture versus its branches

The entropy of the averaged output generally differs from the Born-weighted average of conditional output entropies. Their difference is a nonnegative Holevo quantity bounded by the Shannon entropy of the outcomes. The theorem extends to semifinite algebras under its entropy and support assumptions. Rescaling the trace adds a constant to each entropy, leaving this difference unchanged.

In the eight-clock-sector finite example the averaged entropy change is +0.24903 and the branch average is −0.70330. Their difference 0.95232 equals the outcome entropy numerically. Noisy measure-and-prepare operations can instead increase branch entropy: the information gain can be negative even though the Holevo difference is not.

Identifying the trace entropy with gravitational generalized entropy requires a separately justified relation and controlled clock–field corrections. The finite clock analogue is not a continuum Type II factor. The generalized second law for nested horizon regions does not assert that every quantum channel increases entropy.

4. What happens when an encoding fails to preserve norms?

An isometry preserves quantum norms. For a non-isometric dictionary, the pulled-back measurement weights may sum to more or less than one; a vector in its kernel has zero weight for every outcome. On an approximate-isometry code subspace, the paper gives two uniformly sharp bounds, one for raw weights and another after renormalization. At squared-norm error ε=0.2 the bounds on the sum of absolute deviations are about 0.2010 and 0.2020. Two-dimensional examples attain both; the normalized bound is twice total variation distance.

A genuine sequential unitary evaporation circuit leaves the previously emitted radiation marginal unchanged when the next qubit is emitted. Separately normalized postselected snapshots are different objects. The six-qubit sequential example transfers information to radiation and obeys the unassisted bottleneck; kernel-bearing dictionaries do not themselves define deterministic physical channels.

5. Five tests for a branch-coupling model

The paper separates positive states, linear complete positivity, normalization or success probability, independence of ensemble decomposition, and spatial semicausality. A nonlinear normalized positive map can distinguish two different decompositions of the same state. Conversely, SWAP is a linear unitary channel but transmits information from one subsystem to another. Local trace-preserving operations satisfy spatial no-signaling.

When many unit-norm branch vectors are encoded into a smaller space, they cannot all remain orthogonal. The Welch inequality gives a minimum root-mean-square overlap, not a dynamical transition probability or rate. For 64 vectors in dimension 8 the lower bound is 1/3; a duplicated-frame example gives 0.88976, showing there is no universal matching upper bound.

The paper also tests a local linear classical–quantum coefficient condition and constructs a finite two-clock channel on a zero-total-constraint sector. Dephasing that commutes with the constraint leaves that sector invariant. Neither example derives a physical gravitational coupling rate.

Evidence and remaining questions

The manuscript includes 31 pages, 71 references and 4 original figures. Its supplied Lean project records 61 audited declarations; five numerical suites contain 329 scientific numeric fields. Mathematical results and numerical examples are distinguished from established prior literature and physical hypotheses. The semifinite entropy proof is analytical, not a continuum Lean formalization.

Open problems include relativistic record gluing, a gravitational instrument preserving the crossed-product corner, bounds on the clock–field correction, autonomous matrix evaporation and dynamical branch-transition rates. The three OSTQD papers form a sequence of conditional constructions, not a completed theory of quantum gravity.
