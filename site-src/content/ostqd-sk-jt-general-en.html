Open quantum dynamics near black holes: a guide for general readers

The second study in the OSTQD series asks what remains consistent when quantum observers exchange energy with an environment.

TL;DR

A radiating black hole cannot be modeled only by energy-conserving subsystem dephasing. The paper studies a thermal extension of OSTQD, fluctuation statistics in a two-dimensional model, and the consequences of disturbing correlations across a free-field horizon.

Two central conditional results: JT fluctuation statistics distinguish three specified closures and admit a non-explosive energy jump process with exact detailed balance; the smooth-band chiral defect admits a constructive positive-type point-contact extension with a sharp excitation floor. These models are not observations or a completed gravitational theory.

From the first study to thermal gravity

The earlier OSTQD paper asked how two observers' descriptions can be consistent when they have access to different quantum observables. It used finite matrices, reversible clock time t and a non-selective dissipative parameter s. Its strict energy-moment condition required the dissipative jump operators to commute with the energy. A radiating black hole is an open system: energy is transferred to radiation. The continuation explicitly relaxes the subsystem condition instead of claiming that ordinary energy exchange follows from it.

An infalling observer's smooth semiclassical horizon and a distant observer's exterior records are the motivation, not evidence that they inhabit different real spacetimes. The paper treats three separate settings: finite thermal quantum channels, two-dimensional Jackiw–Teitelboim (JT) dilaton gravity coupled to conformal matter, and a free chiral field across a horizon. A static four-dimensional scattering calculation and finite quantum-memory examples provide additional comparisons.

What an open system can and cannot tell us

When a small system interacts with an environment, observing only the system gives a reduced quantum state. The Schwinger–Keldysh (SK) description represents the forward and backward histories of the density matrix; it separates the bath's response from its fluctuations. The fluctuation–dissipation relation links thermal noise to absorption and emission. The familiar fact that a warm object both absorbs and emits is a useful analogy, but the relation here is a quantum correlation identity.

Three standard conditions on an influence functional preserve normalization, conjugation symmetry and pointwise decay. They are necessary, not a complete test of whether the resulting map is a valid quantum channel. The paper constructs a three-level example passing these pointwise checks whose correlation matrix nevertheless has a negative eigenvalue (about −0.07459). A physical channel must preserve positivity even when the system is entangled with an untouched reference.

Tracing out a bath gives an ensemble description, not a unique account of individual events. The same channel may be represented by two different sets of conditional operations; the numerical example changes one outcome probability from 1 to 0.36 without changing the channel. Nothing in the ensemble law selects which decomposition is physically realized.

Thermal relaxation: order can increase

A warm bath causes upward and downward energy transitions. At a fixed temperature their ratio is fixed by the Boltzmann factor. This allows ordinary clock evolution and auxiliary evolution to commute even when the subsystem exchanges energy. A state can become more pure while approaching a low-temperature equilibrium: increasing disorder of the subsystem is not a universal law for an open system.

In the six-level calculation, purity increases from 0.1667 to 0.5048, while two measures of distance to the Gibbs state decrease. The chosen unprojected two-level Redfield equation, in contrast, develops negative short-time Choi eigenvalues. This is not a claim that every non-secular master equation fails.

During evaporation the bath temperature changes. Instantaneous compatibility does not make a rectangular path through the two time parameters path independent; the change of the generator matters. Nor does slow temperature variation guarantee that the system can keep up: its own relaxation time must be short enough. Comparing a damped subsystem with its free unitary history is also different from comparing it with the full interacting system and bath.

Uniform relaxation controls the discrepancy from a moving Gibbs state. In a weak-coupling example the trace distance remains 0.14973 despite very slow bath variation.

The evaporating black hole is a fluctuating system

JT gravity is a two-dimensional model of gravity, useful for studying near-extremal black holes and boundary dynamics. In the vacuum-bath approximation considered here, the temperature decreases exponentially, while the energy above extremality falls twice as fast in the exponent. Radiation transfers energy stochastically, so a mean cooling curve does not determine the fluctuations.

The mean drift and noise are distinct: an elementary three-level system can have zero mean energy change and a growing energy variance. This matters when comparing a gravitational energy-balance equation with the statistics of radiation. In the JT model, the leading equilibrium Gaussian variance agrees with thermodynamics, but this does not settle the higher moments.

A cumulant summarizes a feature of a probability distribution: the second is variance, the third describes asymmetry and the fourth helps distinguish it from Gaussian noise. The paper solves all cumulants for a particular model in which the noise coefficients follow the deterministic temperature curve. This is like calculating random departures from a predicted cooling trajectory. Restoring the feedback of the actual random energy on the temperature changes the leading third cumulant; even its sign need not remain negative.

The ratios compare three specified approximations with the same energy drift, not every possible Gaussian model. The third includes the full fluctuation symmetry; a Gaussian model with a different drift can have a different stationary law.

For the example C=12 and c=24, the three controls fail at different times: CT reaches 1 at u=187.36, relative energy fluctuations reach 1 at u=308.98, and the characteristic strong-coupling scale occurs at u=412.24. Large fluctuations and failure of the semiclassical approximation make late-time extrapolation unreliable.

The constructed entropy-difference jump process never emits more energy than is available. Its finite entropy-difference weight gives exact detailed balance for the canonical density on nonnegative energy. The stationary partition function is explicit; this is not a controlled late-time vacuum evaporation model.

When a horizon correlation is damaged

In the specified free chiral-current model, exterior number dephasing alters cross-horizon coherence while leaving same-side correlators unchanged. The point-split difference grows as a positive constant times ε⁻², so it is not Hadamard. This does not define a measured local stress tensor.

For a nonzero smooth rate supported strictly within a positive-frequency band, the radial-cutoff raw detector response converges. Nonetheless it turns negative at large gap: convergence is not positivity.

A Mellin-space construction gives a Hermitian positive-type distribution by adding a zero-order point-contact term. The sharp coefficient is c*=0.07443467 for the illustrated profile, greater than the Gaussian-test witness 0.00197949. Zero-order positive extensions require and suffice with c≥c*; any positive finite-order contact correction needs at least c*. The corresponding quasifree CCR state preserves the commutator and is non-normal in the global full-line Unruh Fock representation. It is an explicit non-Hadamard state of this free model, not a gravitational completion.

A uniform dephasing profile instead gives logarithmic raw-cutoff divergence. More general corrections supported along the horizon axes and microscopic rates remain open.

Single outcomes, remote correlations, and no-signaling

For a bosonic pair, exterior number measurement gives every outcome a positive excitation of the infalling quadratic energy, at least 2ωn, where n is the thermal occupation. This is a statement about one measurement procedure. Random phase operations can create the same ensemble dephasing while conditional excitation approaches zero for some branches. The ensemble cannot decide between them.

Local measurements may change a conditional remote state when an outcome is known, a standard quantum effect called steering. Without that outcome, the average remote marginal is unchanged. The paper separates this no-signaling property from extra proposed requirements for observer-specific outcomes and consistency of records. Those extra requirements are not derived from SK dynamics.

Why closing a horizon port is not a simple solution

For a coupled thermal channel, the same spectrum determines both emission and absorption. If the spectrum is set to zero, both rates and the thermal noise vanish. Closing one port does not extinguish radiation through other ports, and the entire Unruh state is not at one thermal equilibrium because incoming and outgoing sectors differ.

The static four-dimensional scattering calculation finds that absorption shrinks at fixed physical frequency as the horizon area tends to zero. Tracking the changing thermal spectrum is different: keeping frequency times the Schwarzschild radius fixed can retain a large transmission probability, about 0.9089 in the scalar example. Neither limit describes the final stage of a real evaporating black hole.

A separate quantum-memory model proves that if every input must pass through a one-dimensional space, the decoded state is independent of the input. An unknown two-level state's entanglement fidelity is exactly 1/4, or 1/16 for four dimensions. The calculation also studies a non-isometric map from 64 to 32 dimensions, which has a null space and is not by itself a trace-preserving physical evolution. The JT residual entropy S₀, the shrinking 4D area and the one-dimensional memory are three distinct endpoints.

The isolated horizon-origin transmitted port is distinct from the full distant output mode: transmission suppression kills the effective horizon-origin kernels but need not remove an exterior incoming/reflected contribution.

What is established, and what remains open

The supplied manuscript includes 94 cited references, 4 vector figures, 40 Lean declarations and six numerical suites covering 309 recorded scientific numbers. The paper attributes its established ingredients to the prior literature; the focused analytical additions concern the frozen-coefficient JT cumulants and the smooth-band horizon distribution and positivity floor. The proof assistant covers stated algebraic and ODE lemmas, not the entire continuum physics. The plots are computed model results, not astronomical data.

A microscopic bath and dephasing rate, a controlled strong-coupling endpoint, a gravitational interpretation of the free-current Mellin state, and general observer instruments remain open. The third study explores selective instruments and their limits.
