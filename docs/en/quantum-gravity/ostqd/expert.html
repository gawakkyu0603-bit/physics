TL;DR — The essentials

For each O, OSTQD assumes a unital accessible algebra A_O, Hamiltonian H_O, and a GKSL dissipator with jumps in A_O, imposing conservation of the first two H_O moments. The core results concern carré du champ and integrability, a causal-local common-commutant obstruction, sharp joint-extension and bottleneck fidelities, and spectral pinching with nonstationary protected windows. The 8-qubit evaporation and SU(2) Galerkin examples are finite benchmarks; gravitational dynamics, single-shot records, and continuum completion are not derived.

A finite-dimensional observer-indexed formulation of reversible clock evolution and non-selective auxiliary dissipation. The following overview distinguishes the paper's exact conditional statements from the physical identifications that motivate them.

Operational motivation and definitions (§§1–2)

The observer discrepancy is framed in terms of accessible records: finite infalling proper time and smooth semiclassical crossing versus redshifted exterior signals and Hawking radiation. Regular coordinates remove the Schwarzschild coordinate singularity; different access does not itself imply separate physical spacetimes.

Let H_O=H_O†∈M_d(C), A_O⊂M_d(C) a unital C*-algebra, L_O,k∈A_O, and ℒ_O=Σ_k D[L_O,k]. The unitary group is U_O(t)(ρ)=e^(−itH_O)ρe^(itH_O) and Φ_O(s)=e^(sℒ_O), s≥0. The joint PDE is integrable when [ℒ_O,ad_HO]=0, yielding ρ_O(t,s)=U_O(t)Φ_O(s)ρ₀. The same commutation extends to piecewise time-dependent H_O(t) with a t-independent dissipator commuting on every interval. Nonintegrable cross-perspective application of Φ_B to H_A uses fixed t or an explicitly ordered composition. The total state, reduced state, and conditional instrument outputs are distinct objects. Neither an objective stochastic unraveling nor a unique relation s(τ) is supplied.

Theorem 1: exact identities and integrability (§3)

The dissipation form is Γ_ℒ(X,Y)=ℒ†(X†Y)−X†ℒ†(Y)−ℒ†(X†)Y. The GKSL expansion gives Γ_ℒ(H,H)=Σ_k[L_k,H]†[L_k,H]. With D=ℒ†(H),

d Varρ(H)/ds = Tr(ρΓ_ℒ(H,H)) + Tr(ρ{H,D}) − 2⟨H⟩ρ⟨D⟩ρ

Thus ℒ†(H)=ℒ†(H²)=0 iff [L_k,H]=0 for all k, and this implies [ℒ,ad_H]=0. The trace of Γ divided by d equals the average initial variance derivative over an orthonormal H eigenbasis, not the derivative at the maximally mixed density matrix. An example with L=σ_z,H=σ_x gives Γ expectation 4 and covariance −4 at I/2. An independent nonzero-D example has H=diag(0,1,2), L=|2⟩⟨1|, ‖D‖_F=1, and zero drift covariance at the specified state. A nonunital flow's fixed observable condition alone need not imply commutation with jumps.

Theorem 2: local obstruction, not global noncommutativity (§3)

For d≥2, if A∩{H_A,H_B}′=CI, any simultaneously BPS-immune dissipator with jumps in A is zero. For H_B-immune jumps, write L̃_k=L_k−Tr(L_k)I/d. On the traceless local H_B commutant define γ_loc as the smallest Rayleigh quotient of ad_HA†ad_HA. Then

Tr Γ_ℒB(H_A,H_A)/d ≥ (γ_loc/d) Σₖ ‖L̃_B,k‖_F²

The statement is a dissipation-form and eigenbasis-averaged initial variance bound, not a universal lower bound for the variance derivative of an arbitrary mixed state. In the two-level Bogoliubov test γ=ω²sin²θ. For H_B=αH_A with nonzero real α the clock conflict vanishes on any nonempty traceless commutant, even though visibility of other observables can decay.

For the two-sided fermionic Hawking pair, use H_B=ω(N_R−N_L) and H_A=ω(a_1†a_1+a_2†a_2), with tan r=e^(−ω/(2T_H)). The Jordan–Wigner representation gives [H_A,H_B]=0 exactly on M₄(C); the bilocal jump H_B is nontrivial and immune to both. Exterior-locality A_B=M₂(C)_R⊗I_L forces a traceless H_B-commuting X to be proportional to N_R−I/2. For ω≠0 and nonzero sin r cos r,

γ_loc = 8ω² sin²r cos²r = 8ω² n_FD(1−n_FD)

With L=√Γ N_R, the initial inertial vacuum cos r|00⟩+sin r|11⟩ evolves by multiplying the off-diagonal pair coherence by e^(−Γs/2). The inertial mean energy is 4ω n_FD(1−n_FD)(1−e^(−Γs/2)); for Γ>0 its limiting value divided by 2ω equals ‖ρ₀−P_Bρ₀‖_F²=S_L(ρ_R)=2n_FD(1−n_FD). The single-mode initial derivative for statistics σ=±1 is 2Γω n_σ(1+σn_σ). The bosonic extension uses a finite-support core and a squeezed-vacuum limit, with cutoff numerical checks, not a full formalized unbounded-operator treatment or Hadamard analysis.

Theorem 3: joint extension, sharp witness, and restricted CPL (§4)

For a joint CPTP channel from d_C-dimensional C to A′B′, normalized Choi states have reference marginal I_R/d_C. Let P_A=|Φ⟩⟨Φ|_RA′⊗I_B′ and P_B the exchanged projector. Then P_A P_B P_A=d_C⁻²P_A; the largest eigenvalue of P_A+P_B is 1+1/d_C.

F_A + F_B ≤ 1+1/d_C

The paper's symmetric Choi witness is a normalized projector mixture onto the top eigenspace, with exactly maximally mixed R marginal, giving F_A=F_B=(1+1/d_C)/2. This is a sharp linear envelope, compared in the paper with the inherited Kay–Kaszlikowski–Ramanathan curved monogamy bound. At F_A=1 an independent other output can have F_B=1/d_C², not the linear-envelope intercept 1/d_C.

The separate-channel construction uses reversible channels with explicit inverse decoders and a chosen commuting shared algebra for equality of expectation values. It does not produce a broadcast channel or a simultaneous pair of recoverable copies; it does not implement outcome-level cross-perspective links.

Theorem 4: spectral block decay and exact window protection (§5)

For Hermitian K_k, ℒ=−½Σ_k ad_Kk², and d Trρ²/ds=−Σ_k‖[K_k,ρ]‖_F². For commuting jumps with common spectral projectors P_a and joint eigenvalues κ_ka, the a,b block is an eigenoperator with eigenvalue −½Σ_k(κ_ka−κ_kb)².

Φ(s)(P_aρP_b)=exp[−s Σₖ(κₖa−κₖb)²/2] P_aρP_b

If distinct sectors are separated, Φ(s) converges to pinching P. Since P is an orthogonal projector for the Hilbert–Schmidt product, S_L(Pρ)=S_L(ρ)+‖ρ−Pρ‖_F². Static entropy is insufficient to infer the history of decoherence. The fixed-point invisibility statements are restricted to their stated jump/flow assumptions. For a finite-width spectral window Π_win=1_[E−Δ,E+Δ](H_B), jumps scalar on the window make all supported states s-invariant while an intra-window Hamiltonian can generate nontrivial t evolution.

Theorem 5: sharp intermediate dimension bound (§5)

Let E and R be CPTP with Kraus A_i:C→f, B_j:f→C. The expression F_e=d_C⁻²Σ_ij|Tr(B_jA_i)|², trace preservation, and Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz yield

F_e(R∘E) ≤ min(1,d_f²/d_C²)

Sharpness for d_f≤d_C is witnessed by transmitting a d_f-dimensional subspace isometrically and resetting its orthogonal complement, with an isometric decoder. The non-preserved complementary Kraus terms have zero trace in the composite fidelity expression. When d_f≥d_C an isometric embedding with a left inverse gives 1. The possible physical identification ln d_f with a black-hole entropy is an additional assumption, not part of the theorem.

Two-window evaporation and companion matrix calculation (§6)

The finite model has N=8 qubits and a reference-entangled input code d_C=2. Piecewise time-dependent unitary evolution on the initially occupied window, with coarse-grained jumps scalar there, gives exact s-invariance in the stated model. Sequential emissions define radiation channels and complementary remnant channels; Petz transpose recovery provides an explicit comparison. The radiation entropy upper bound min(k,9−k) follows from the purity of the complete N+1-qubit system. At k=4,5 the reported entropy is 3.5720,3.6294 bits; at k=8 it is 1 bit. Radiation and remnant fidelities end at 1 and 0.25. The recorded maximum sum 1.250543 is below 1.5. The bottleneck is binding only at complete emission in this example, since d_f=2^(8−k).

The SU(2) Yang–Mills companion Galerkin calculation distinguishes projection of the full Hamiltonian from products of truncated operators. For the four-dimensional singlet sector at cutoff 3, the reported energies are 2.1436, 3.6063, 3.6063, 4.2664; the difference between the projected Hamiltonian matrices is 1.3856 in Frobenius norm. This is a finite numerical benchmark, not evidence for chaos or a large-N evaporation limit.

Physical scope and open constructions (§7)

The inherited inputs include GKSL dynamics, Bogoliubov transformations, quantum cloning monogamy, pinching, and Petz recovery. The observer-indexed framework combines these with explicit local and dimension-restricted conditional constructions. Engineered two-mode squeezing and dephasing can test the channel identities; a genuine gravitational deviation would need a microscopic Γ_eff(τ)=Γ_O ds/dτ prediction. Infinite-dimensional crossed-product algebras, renormalized continuum horizon stress-energy and Hadamard conditions, selective instruments and causal record merging, large-N BFSS/IKKT rate derivation, and emergence of a common Lorentzian spacetime remain open.

Formal statements and analytic scope (§8)

The supplied Lean library contains 38 declarations: GKSL basics (2), energy moments (7), local horizon results (13), joint extension and CPL (6), purity and energy windows (6), bottleneck (4). The manuscript's Table 3 records the distinctions between algebraic identities, the fermionic entrywise ODE, and steps outside the formalization. In particular, the matrix-exponential passage and the variance chain rule, the infimum defining the local gap, the bosonic Fock-space extension, the curved monogamy inequality, the analytic semigroup limit, and the isometric saturation for larger intermediate spaces are not all end-to-end formalized. The supplied kernel log is evidence about the recorded build, not an empirical confirmation of the proposed dynamics.

The von Neumann counterpart of the pinching identity is S(Pρ)=S(ρ)+D(ρ∥Pρ); unlike the Hilbert–Schmidt linear-entropy identity, it uses relative entropy. For generic nonunital GKSL flow, conservation of a single expectation does not identify the full jump commutant. The paper discusses these restrictions and the remaining continuum and operational problems.
