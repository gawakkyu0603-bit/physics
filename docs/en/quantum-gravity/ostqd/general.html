TL;DR — The essentials

OSTQD proposes two independent descriptions of change: ordinary reversible quantum evolution in clock time and a directed auxiliary dephasing process for an observer's accessible records. Five conditional mathematical results delimit when energies can be conserved, when local dephasing disturbs a horizon vacuum, how two recovery channels can or cannot coexist, how coherence is lost, and how small memories limit quantum information. The framework is a research proposal, not a verified theory of gravity.

What can two observers actually learn about the same black hole? The proposal starts with a question about access to physical records rather than a claim that one observer's spacetime literally disappears.

Why a black hole presents an unusual question

In semiclassical general relativity, a person falling toward a large black hole can cross the horizon in finite time measured by their own clock. An observer far away cannot see that crossing directly; signals reaching the exterior are increasingly redshifted. Hawking's calculation predicts radiation, so an exterior description of an evaporating black hole has a different set of accessible records. Different coordinate labels alone are not a contradiction, and the model does not establish the absence of an interior.

OSTQD uses this difference in access as motivation. It asks which observables and transformations are physically available to each perspective.

Two parameters, two different operations

Ordinary quantum evolution rotates a state without destroying information in the total closed system. A subsystem can nevertheless appear mixed because information is entangled with another system. The proposed auxiliary parameter s describes a separate, non-selective dephasing process: phases become unavailable in the ensemble, but this operation does not decide which individual measurement outcome occurs.

For a two-level state with equal amplitudes, complete dephasing replaces the coherent superposition by equal probabilities. A later interference experiment distinguishes them. The paper does not identify s with a second physical clock or provide a microscopic law for how s advances in nature.

1. Can dephasing preserve energy?

A change can preserve average energy while changing energy fluctuations. Requiring preservation of both the first and second energy moments for every input state forces every jump operator in the model to commute with the energy observable. This also makes the two operations compatible: applying ordinary evolution then dephasing gives the same result as the opposite order.

The variance contribution from dephasing is generally accompanied by energy drift. In one example an equally mixed two-level state has zero actual variance change even though the dissipation contribution is 4; the energy-drift contribution is −4. Averaging the initial variance rates of individual energy eigenstates instead gives 4.

2. Why restrictions on available operations matter

An exterior observer cannot implement an operation requiring direct access to both partners of a horizon pair. In a four-dimensional fermionic pair, the two observers' energy operators commute when unrestricted two-sided operations are allowed. Once the exterior observer is restricted to one partner, nontrivial dephasing conserving both energies is impossible under the stated algebraic conditions.

For a mode of energy ω, thermal occupation n, and dephasing rate Γ, the initial energy increase assigned by the infalling energy operator to the exterior-dephased state is 2Γω n(1−n) for fermions, or 2Γω n(1+n) for bosons. These are channel-model predictions, not measurements of actual horizon heating.

When Γ is positive, the fermionic limiting excited-pair number is 2n(1−n), precisely equal to the linear entropy of its thermal partner. A simple proportional change of clock rate does not produce this incompatibility.

3. Can both observers recover an unknown quantum state?

A quantum state is not a classical file that can be copied perfectly. If two recovery devices are outputs of one common quantum channel, their entanglement fidelities F_A and F_B obey a bound. For a two-level input their sum cannot exceed 1.5.

The model also considers separate reversible descriptions, each of which can in principle recover the state perfectly. This is not a device that gives two perfect copies simultaneously: a joint extension of the two recoveries would violate the bound. Agreement is required only for expectations of chosen shared commuting observables.

4. Coherence, entropy, and protected motion

Imagine sorting quantum states into energy bands. Dephasing can erase phases between different bands while leaving all states inside one band unchanged. In a protected band the ordinary dynamics may still change the state, so the absence of auxiliary dephasing does not mean the system stands still.

For Hermitian jump operators the purity of the total state decreases or stays constant along s. When the operation completely removes the cross-band terms, the increase in linear entropy equals the squared size of those removed terms. The entropy of a state alone cannot reveal which physical process originally made it mixed.

5. A smaller memory limits recoverable quantum information

If every piece of quantum information must travel through a memory with fewer dimensions than the input, perfect recovery is impossible in general. For input dimension d_C and intermediate dimension d_f, the maximum entanglement fidelity is min(1,(d_f/d_C)²). A four-dimensional input passing through a two-dimensional memory has a maximum of 1/4; through a one-dimensional memory it is 1/16.

The proof assumes a genuine quantum encoding and decoding through that memory alone. If another system can carry information around the bottleneck, its dimension must be accounted for.

What the evaporation example shows

The manuscript follows an eight-qubit model of a young black hole plus a reference qubit. One qubit at a time becomes radiation. The radiation entropy reaches 3.5720 bits at the fourth step and 3.6294 at the fifth, ending at one bit because the reference is still entangled. Radiation recovery rises from 0.25 to 1, while remnant recovery falls from 1 to 0.25. These numbers are simulated, not astronomical observations.

A separate finite matrix benchmark for SU(2) Yang–Mills dynamics finds four low levels, including a degenerate doublet. It checks the numerical projection method, not the behavior of a real black hole.

What would turn the idea into a physical theory?

Controlled two-mode quantum experiments could check the dephasing and recovery formulas. They would test the channel model, not establish gravitational actualization. A microscopic relation between auxiliary time and ordinary time, continuum horizon energy, a rule for individual outcomes and merging records, and an emergent spacetime construction remain open. The mathematical proofs and numerical examples describe consequences of explicit assumptions; they do not demonstrate that nature realizes those assumptions.

What mathematical checking can establish

The manuscript reports machine-checked finite-dimensional algebraic results in Lean and independent numerical calculations of the same models. A proof of a statement about matrices does not prove that black holes obey the assumed dynamics. Section 8 separates the algebraic proof, analytic steps and the remaining physical hypotheses.

OSTQD uses established ideas including open quantum systems, Hawking pairs, quantum-information recovery, and decoherence-free subspaces. Its contribution is to put these ingredients into an observer-indexed framework with precise conditional restrictions.
