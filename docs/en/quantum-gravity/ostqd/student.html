TL;DR — The essentials

The framework assigns each observer a Hamiltonian, accessible algebra, and GKSL semigroup. Conservation of both energy moments is equivalent to commuting jumps; a local common-commutant obstruction, joint-channel fidelity tradeoff, spectral pinching identity, and dimension bottleneck follow under specified hypotheses. Numerical examples illustrate these finite-dimensional statements, not a continuum theory.

Start from density matrices and finite-dimensional channels. The paper uses familiar quantum mechanics to make the observer-access question precise, then asks which conclusions follow from the additional auxiliary dynamics.

Observer perspectives and two-time evolution

Let H be a Hermitian Hamiltonian on a finite d-dimensional Hilbert space, and ρ a positive density matrix of trace 1. An observer O is assigned an accessible unital C*-subalgebra A_O, Hamiltonian H_O, and jumps L_k in A_O. Physical time t is reversible; auxiliary s ≥ 0 is a directed parameter.

∂ₜρ = −i[H,ρ], ∂ₛρ = ℒ(ρ)

ℒ(ρ) = Σₖ (LₖρLₖ† − ½{Lₖ†Lₖ,ρ})

The solution U(t)Φ(s)(ρ₀) is path independent if [ℒ,ad_H] = 0. Otherwise mixed derivatives need not agree. The total unitary state and its reduced state after a partial trace must be distinguished. An ensemble CPTP map is not a selective instrument; a record-level rule would need a decomposition into outcome-indexed maps.

Theorem 1: dissipation and the two energy moments

The Heisenberg dual ℒ† acts on observables. For Γ_ℒ(X,Y) = ℒ†(X†Y) − X†ℒ†(Y) − ℒ†(X†)Y, expansion gives

Γ_ℒ(H,H) = Σₖ [Lₖ,H]†[Lₖ,H] ≥ 0

If both ℒ†(H) and ℒ†(H²) vanish, each positive term must vanish. Conversely commuting jumps conserve both moments and imply [ℒ,ad_H] = 0. For D = ℒ†(H), the variance derivative also contains the symmetrized drift covariance.

∂ₛ Varρ(H) = Tr(ρΓ_ℒ(H,H)) + Tr(ρ{H,D}) − 2 Tr(ρH)Tr(ρD)

For an energy eigenstate the covariance vanishes; the average of these eigenstate rates equals Tr Γ_ℒ(H,H)/d. It is not the variance derivative of the mixed state I/d. Example: H = σ_x, L = σ_z, ρ = I/2 gives 0 = 4 − 4. With H = σ_z, L = σ_x and |+⟩, the noncommuting-PDE residual has Frobenius norm 2√2.

Theorem 2: the local algebra is decisive

The commutant of an observable consists of matrices commuting with it. If A ∩ {H_A,H_B}′ contains only scalars, no nontrivial GKSL dissipator with jumps in A can conserve both observers' first and second energy moments.

γ_loc = inf ‖[X,H_A]‖_F² / ‖X‖_F² (X ≠ 0, Tr X = 0, X ∈ A ∩ {H_B}′)

In a two-level example, H_B = ωσ_z/2 and H_A = ω(cos θ σ_z + sin θ σ_x)/2 give γ = ω²sin²θ. For a nonzero proportional redshift H_B = αH_A the commutants coincide and γ is zero when the traceless domain is nonempty.

For a fermionic pair, H_B = ω(N_R−N_L), and H_A is the inertial-mode number Hamiltonian. Globally the two commute. Restricted to exterior matrices X = c(N_R−I/2), however,

n = 1/(e^(ω/T_H)+1), γ_loc = 8ω²n(1−n)

⟨H_A⟩(s) = 4ωn(1−n)(1−e^(−Γs/2))

For Γ>0, dividing the saturated energy by 2ω gives 2n(1−n), the linear entropy of the reduced Hawking state. For bosons replace the Fermi occupation by n_BE and the initial excitation factor by n_BE(1+n_BE); the paper checks a truncated Fock calculation. This one-mode result does not determine a renormalized continuum stress tensor.

Theorem 3: one joint channel versus separate channels

Entanglement fidelity measures recovery by comparing the decoded half of a maximally entangled reference–input state with the original pair. For a common joint CPTP extension to A′ and B′, the projectors onto the reference–A′ and reference–B′ entangled targets satisfy P_A P_B P_A = P_A/d_C².

F_A + F_B ≤ 1 + 1/d_C

The norm of P_A+P_B yields the linear ceiling. The explicit Choi witness in the paper has the required maximally mixed reference marginal and saturates the ceiling symmetrically. For d_C=2, the optimum symmetric fidelities are 0.75. The known curved cloning tradeoff imposes an additional restriction away from the symmetric point. Separate reversible maps with inverse decoders give fidelity 1 in their own descriptions and expectation agreement on a chosen commuting algebra, not a common joint extension.

Theorem 4: purity, pinching, and energy windows

For Hermitian jumps K_k, ℒ(ρ)=−½Σ_k[K_k,[K_k,ρ]]. Trace cyclicity yields

∂ₛ Trρ² = −Σₖ ‖[Kₖ,ρ]‖_F² ≤ 0

For commuting jumps with joint spectral blocks P_a, the a,b block decays as exp[−s Σ_k(κ_ka−κ_kb)²/2]. If distinct sectors are separated by at least one jump, the limit is the Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal pinching P(ρ)=Σ_a P_aρP_a. The Pythagorean identity is

S_L(Pρ) = S_L(ρ) + ‖ρ−Pρ‖_F², S_L(ρ)=1−Trρ²

A finite energy window is an exactly protected subspace if every jump is scalar on it. A Hamiltonian preserving that window can still generate nonstationary internal evolution. A narrow window is not necessarily a single energy eigenstate.

Theorem 5: a sharp channel bottleneck

For encoding E:C→f and decoding R:f→C, let A_i and B_j be their Kraus matrices, with both channels trace preserving. The entanglement fidelity of the composition is d_C⁻²Σ_ij|Tr(B_jA_i)|².

F_e(R∘E) ≤ min(1,d_f²/d_C²)

The bound is sharp, attained by identity transport on a d_f-dimensional subspace with reset of the complement when d_f≤d_C. It is independent of the number of Kraus operators.

Two-window evaporation and a Galerkin benchmark

The toy system starts with N=8 black-hole qubits and a reference entangled with a one-qubit code. Piecewise unitary dynamics within a protected window emit one qubit at a time; s evolution is invariant on the populated window. The radiation entropy is bounded by min(k,9−k) bits. At k=4 and 5 the recorded entropies are 3.5720 and 3.6294 bits. Petz recovery from radiation rises from 0.25 to 1; the complementary remnant recovery drops to 0.25. The maximum recorded joint sum is 1.250543, below 1.5.

For d_C=2 and d_f=2^(8−k) the dimension ceiling is 1 until k=8, then 1/4. Earlier loss of remnant fidelity is entanglement transfer, not a tightening of the bottleneck ceiling. In the independent SU(2) Yang–Mills Galerkin example, the singlet energies at cutoff 3 are about 2.1436, 3.6063, 3.6063, 4.2664. Projection of the full operator is not equivalent to multiplying individually truncated operators.

Where the mathematical model ends

The finite-dimensional results can be compared with standard open-system and quantum-information tools. Reproducing them in engineered squeezing/dephasing experiments would test these channels. To make a gravitational prediction one needs a microscopic rate Γ_eff=Γ_O ds/dτ, a continuum stress tensor and regularity analysis, selective instruments with a causal record-merging rule, and a consistent emergent geometry. The paper's algebraic results do not establish those ingredients.

Mathematical provenance and proof scope

The supplied Lean library formalizes the finite-dimensional algebraic core in 38 theorem and lemma declarations across six modules. The manuscript reports a successful kernel build and standard axioms only. The matrix-exponential integrability step, the derivative chain rule for the variance, bosonic infinite-dimensional limits, the analytic limit to pinching, and physical interpretation are distinguished from the machine-checked statements in section 8.

The conditional finite-dimensional results inherit GKSL theory, conservation criteria, Bogoliubov modes, quantum cloning monogamy, Petz recovery, and decoherence-free subspaces. The paper connects these tools; it does not replace them or derive a full theory of quantum gravity.
